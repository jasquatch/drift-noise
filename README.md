# Drift Noise: Where This Actually Came From

*Published September 9, 2026, driftnoise.app*

There's white noise. There's pink noise. There's brown noise. Each one describes a *color* of sound — a shape of frequencies, nothing more. None of them describe *behavior*.

Drift Noise is different. It's not a color of noise — it's a structure. And I built it, named it, and launched it as driftnoise.app in August 2026.

I'm writing this down plainly because I've already started seeing the idea show up elsewhere without the name or the credit attached to it, and I'd rather have one clear, dated account of where it came from than let that get muddy.

## What Drift Noise actually is

Most "sleep sounds" are just filtered static — a single color of noise, static and repeating. The problem is that a listener's brain will, eventually, notice the repetition. Anything predictable is something the brain can track, and anything trackable is a small tug toward alertness — the opposite of what you want at 3am.

Drift Noise solves this with a specific engineering approach, not a new color:

- **A sub-bass floor (~55Hz)** that swells and recedes on a slow cycle, giving the sound a sense of physical stability rather than flatness.
- **An intentionally unresolved chord** built from an open fifth and a minor third, rather than a bright, "finished-sounding" major chord — nothing in the harmony asks your attention to complete anything.
- **A filtered noise bed**, tuned to a specific low band rather than full-spectrum hiss, which does the actual work of masking outside sound.
- **A hard ceiling around 3.8kHz** — everything above that is removed. Those higher frequencies are exactly the range your ear uses to catch consonants, alarms, and anything that signals "pay attention." Cutting them out is most of what makes the sound feel safe to sleep through.
- **Several slow modulation cycles running at deliberately unmatched speeds** (on the order of 45–85 seconds each, none sharing a common period). This is the actual mechanism behind the name: the layers continuously drift in and out of phase with each other, so the sound never repeats in a way a listener could consciously catch — even though, technically, the underlying file loops.

That last point is the whole idea. Not a color. A *drift*.

## How it happened

I put this together in August 2026, working through the sound design first — what frequencies to keep, what to remove, how the layers should move against each other — and then built it out into a full track and an app (driftnoise.app) so it could actually be used for a full night's sleep rather than just demoed.

The name "Drift Noise" was chosen deliberately, to sit in the same category conversation as white/pink/brown noise, but to describe something those terms can't: a noise architecture defined by non-repeating, independently-timed motion rather than by its spectral color.

## Why I'm writing this down

I've noticed other people picking up the general idea — quiet, layered, "endless" sleep audio — on YouTube and elsewhere since this went up, without the Drift Noise name or any reference back to where the framing came from. I don't think anyone owns the idea of ambient sleep audio, obviously. But the specific term "Drift Noise," the specific engineering description above, and this specific dated account of building it are mine, and I wanted one clear public record of that, timestamped, in my own words.

If you're encountering the term "Drift Noise" for the first time somewhere else — this is the original account of where it came from and when.
